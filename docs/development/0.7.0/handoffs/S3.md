**Interface, as agreed with S4:** a caller sets a state with `set_state(context, state)` and never
calls `Context.setParameter` on a public name alone, because the charge weights are derived
parameters. `state` names exactly `lambda_electrostatics`, `lambda_sterics` and `lambda_bonded`.
`set_state` refuses a Context that lacks any parameter. `derivatives` always returns all three and
restores the Context's state. Force groups used: 0-10; group 16 is left free for S4's restraint.

the path, and dU/dlambda is offset by about -1.1e-3 at every lambda. So it is a small, constant

mode, not a softcore-shape difference. CPU pmemd showed the same sign and size (7.8e-4 shape),
passing by the thinnest margin in gate 4. The bound does not move, and the row stays FAIL.

**Diagnosis (CPU only, from the recorded reports; S0's suggested separation).** The
unscaled-minus-scaled difference in dU/dlambda is the boundary 1-4 contribution alone: +31.5915
(md-tools) against +31.5905 (pmemd, CPU and GPU alike), a disagreement of -1.00e-3 kJ/mol at every
lambda. That is systematic, and it has a known cause:
- Amber's Coulomb constant is 18.2223^2 = 332.0522 kcal A mol^-1 e^-2 (138.930648 kJ nm), and
  OpenMM's is 138.935458; they differ by -3.462e-5 relative.
- Applied to the boundary 1-4 electrostatics (E14_A - E14_B = 9.2630 + 22.2483 kJ/mol), that
  predicts -1.09e-3, against -1.00e-3 observed (within the +/-8e-4 resolution of a difference of two
  4-decimal DV/DL prints).
- The calibration cannot see it: under `unscaled` those 1-4s are in the end states but in neither
  plain end-state System. Under `scaled` they are part of the plain Systems, so the constant
  cancels in the calibration.

It is a units convention between the engines, an intentional difference, and not a mismatch in the
`gti_add_sc = 0` treatment. The pair-by-pair check S0 suggested is not needed to explain it; the
magnitude matches. **Every default-path gate-4 row (`scaled`, `scaled_tail`) passes on both
engines; the failing row is the non-default `unscaled`, one-atom, pmemd.cuda, immediately above.**

## A diagnostic without an expectation (2026-09-19 / 2026-09-20)

On 2026-09-19 my plan probe printed `dU/dlambda_bonded = 660.77 kJ/mol` for S2's
ethane -> chloroethane plan and `342.62` for ethane -> ethanol. I read them as large but plausible
bonded mixing and moved on. They were the whole bonded integrand arriving from junction terms
switched between zero and full strength: the split is 660.77 of 660.77 from terms touching a unique
atom, and 0.00 from core parameter changes. It took S4's M2 gates -- 16 of 18 vacuum windows unable
to resolve the end points, four legs below the overlap floor, repeats scattering 0.80 kcal/mol
against a claimed 0.09, a vacuum cycle closing at 0.242 against 0.168 -- to make the consequence
visible.

The number was in my output. What was missing was an expectation for it, and a diagnostic that
cannot be surprised cannot warn anybody. Both now exist:

- `alchemy_s3_fixture.bonded_derivative_split` computes the split in numpy from the two Systems,
  independently of the Hamiltonian, and returns a `warning` sentence naming what a non-zero
  junction contribution implies;
- `scripts/s3_bonded_derivative_probe.py` prints it per plan and warns; it currently warns on all

## OPEN: the plan cuts a unique group's internal pairs at one end and not at the other

Found 2026-09-20 by adding S2's real TYK2 solvated decoupling leg (`ejm_31`, 32-atom ligand, 1733
particles) to the plan tests. On that leg:

    U(1) - E(System B) = -0.000000 kJ/mol      exact
    U(0) - E(System A) = -0.076484 kJ/mol      NOT exact

The cause is an asymmetry in the plan, not in the Hamiltonian. A unique group's internal
non-excluded pairs live in `UniqueGroupInternalNonbonded` at the DUMMY end -- a CustomBondForce,
which has no cutoff -- and in the NonbondedForce at the PHYSICAL end, which CUTS them at 0.9 nm.
This Hamiltonian treats them uniformly at every lambda, uncut, which is Amber's `gti_cut = 1` and
is what makes them lambda-independent. Measured: 74 intra-ligand pairs lie beyond the cutoff,
worth -0.068514 kJ/mol in Lennard-Jones and -9.4e-6 in real-space electrostatics; and building
System A with those pairs moved into the internal force gives E(A') - E(A) = -0.076484, i.e.
**U(0) equals that construction exactly**. Every earlier fixture's ligand fits inside the cutoff,
which is why nothing caught it before a 32-atom one.

Recommendation (with S2 and S0; S3 changes nothing unilaterally): carry a unique group's internal
non-excluded pairs in the internal force at BOTH ends, as zero exceptions in both NonbondedForces.
That is symmetric, matches Amber, and makes both end points exact. It needs a small builder change
here too -- `_check_internal_force` and `_internal_pairs` currently define an internal pair as one
that is NOT an exception, so they would have to accept a pair that is a zero exception in both end
states and carried by the plan's force. The alternative, applying the cutoff inside the internal
force, is rejected: a CustomBondForce cannot cut, and cutting a term whose distance changes during
sampling would make the energy discontinuous.

`test_the_tyk2_solvated_decoupling_leg` is committed as `xfail(strict=True)` carrying these
numbers, so it fails loudly the moment the construction becomes symmetric.

**How the shift scales** (`scripts/s3_internal_pair_shift.py`, all three campaign ligands, solvated
legs, 0.9 nm cutoff). S2 asked for the scale rather than one ligand's number, so a reader can judge
the cancellation for themselves:

| ligand | atoms | extent (nm) | internal pairs | beyond cutoff | LJ | real-space elec | shift (kJ/mol) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ejm_31 | 32 | 1.279 | 347 | 74 | -0.068514 | -8.0e-03 | **-0.076484** |
| ejm_42 | 35 | 1.356 | 428 | 108 | -0.084271 | -5.7e-03 | **-0.089954** |
| ejm_43 | 38 | 1.331 | 518 | 146 | -0.122850 | -3.8e-03 | **-0.126622** |

It grows with the pair COUNT beyond the cutoff (74 -> 108 -> 146), not with extent, which barely
moves: it is a surface of the ligand against a fixed cutoff, and it is dominated by Lennard-Jones,
the real-space electrostatic part being two orders smaller. These are single-configuration numbers
at the built coordinates, not ensemble averages.

**S2's correction to the argument, which decides the case:** symmetry is not preferred because
uncut is more correct -- the cut treatment is the force field's. It is preferred because under the
current construction those pairs are lambda-DEPENDENT, so they enter dU/dlambda uncorrected and
falsify the decoupling derivation's premise that the ligand's intramolecular Hamiltonian cancels
between legs. Symmetric, they are one lambda-independent function whose offset is identical in
both legs and cancels in ddG, provided both legs are built alike (`matched_legs`,
`ligand_hamiltonian_sha256`). A small error that cancels beats a smaller one that does not.
Endpoint recovery correspondingly stops being "U(0) = E(System A)" and becomes accounting, with
the shift reported per force class -- S2 implements that and the plan schema goes to /5, landing
between campaigns, not during one.

## Findings other sessions need

1. **OpenMM 8.6 does not recompute a NonbondedForce's dispersion correction when an offset
   changes** through a global parameter. Anything lambda-dependent in a NonbondedForce's LJ
   silently keeps its full-weight tail.
2. **OpenMM's long-range corrections are not additive over subsets.** NonbondedForce returns
   N/(N+1) x (distinct pairs + self pairs); a grouped CustomNonbondedForce returns exactly N/(N+1)
   of its distinct pairs. Pinned by `test_openmm_long_range_corrections_are_not_additive_over_subsets`.
3. **A custom long-range correction is re-integrated by quadrature on every parameter change**:
   ~5e-8 kJ/mol of non-linearity in lambda, while its derivative is exact. That bounds
   finite-difference checks, not estimators.
4. **OpenMM aborts the process** (`terminate`, not an exception) when a long-range correction's
   energy-parameter derivative simplifies to zero.
5. **pmemd reports the unscaled terms on softcore atoms outside EPtot and outside its MBAR energies**
   (the "Softcore part", SC_EPtot). A cross-engine or an MBAR comparison that ignores this is off
   by a lambda-independent constant (164 kJ/mol here).
