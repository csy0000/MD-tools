## What exists now

**Configuration.** Three optional `scaler.config` keys, approved by S0 (contract §2):
`backbone_scaling_list`, `sidechain_scaling_list` and
`ligand_scaling_dict: {label: {mask, torsion_exclusions: auto | file}}`. With none of them the
selection is the legacy full solute, byte-identical to 0.6.0. With any of them the selection is
explicit: an omitted category means none, and an empty region is refused. Forwarding the keys
from `configs/md/REST2.config` through `build-md` is S0's and is **not done**.

**Modules**, all under `src/md_tools/rest2/`, which S1 owns:

| module | what it is |
|---|---|
| `masks.py` | `parse_residue_mask`, grammar `md-tools-residue-mask/1`: `":45"`, lists, inclusive ranges and mixtures, one-based topology residue indices. Everything else is refused by name with the supported subset printed |
| `regions.py` | THE resolver (policy `md-tools-selective-rest2/1`): backbone/sidechain membership, the central-bond ownership table, ligand instances, `md-tools-torsion-exclusions/1` files bound to a parameter package, the CMAP rule, and `explicit_selection` joining them into one record |
| `selection.py` | `ScalingSelection` → `md-tools-solute-selection/2.0` (still reads 1.0 as legacy); `topology_digest` canonical (sorted bond set, scheme recorded), with a named 1.0 compatibility branch |
| `hamiltonian.py` | `build_scaled_system(..., torsion_central_bonds=None, cmap_terms=None)`. With neither argument it is the whole-solute rule, unchanged. A selective region refuses scaled impropers and generalised-Born Systems |
| `identity.py` | `md-tools-hamiltonian-identity/v3`, which hashes the Hamiltonian-determining projection of the selection (`hamiltonian_selection_projection`), never its provenance. `_legacy_v2_agrees` is the named v2 branch; `hamiltonian_identities_agree` is its boolean form |

`build/scaler.py` (owned) resolves the region when any key is present, prints the resolved residue
map in `scaler.log`, and records three new keys in `scaler.yaml`:

* `selection`: the 2.0 document;
* `selection_sha256`;
* `scaler_arguments`: exactly the `build_scaled_system` arguments, so every state rebuilds from
  the record alone.

`solute` stays the whole solute that trajectories and restraints use.
`unscaled_torsions.unscaled_central_bonds` is now the list the states were actually built with.
In legacy mode it is identical to before.

**Decisions made here, all recorded in the code docstrings and in
`docs/openmm_methods/REST2/README.md` §"Selective REST2":**

* **Ownership of central bonds.**
  * N–CA and CA–C belong to their residue's backbone.
  * Any bond with a sidechain atom belongs to that residue's sidechain, so χ1 (CA–CB) is the
    sidechain's.
  * The peptide bond C(i)–N(i+1) belongs to residue i's backbone (IUPAC ω_i). This matters only
    for proline-like ω, since ordinary amide ω stays unscaled.
  * A disulfide SG–SG is owned jointly: scaled only when both sidechains are selected, and
    reported when only one is.
  * Any other bond between residues is refused when either side is selected.
* **Membership.**
  * Backbone is N, H, H1–H3, CA, HA, HA2, HA3, C, O and OXT.
  * A cap is backbone throughout.
  * Glycine has no sidechain; proline's ring is sidechain.
  * Nucleic acids and residues outside `PROTEIN_RESIDUES` are refused by name.
* **CMAP.** A term is scaled iff both its φ and ψ central bonds are selected. Under this ownership
  a mask cannot produce a mixed term for a standard residue, so the "mixed" branch is tested on a
  hand-built selected-bond set.
* **Exclusion files add protection and never remove it.** A file with no recorded package on the
  instance, a mismatched `parameters`, or a bond with no proper torsion is refused.
* **The compact form `L01: <path>` is DEFERRED, not dropped (S0 ruling).** It is refused by name
  with the explicit form printed. Adding an instance name to `md-tools-ligand-mapping/1` is S0's
  item.
* **`REST2_IMPLEMENTATION` stays `rest2-unscaled-torsions` v3.** This is a deviation from contract
  §3's original text, accepted by S0 at `746edaa`: bumping it would refuse every 0.6.0
  continuation. Selective semantics are versioned by the selection policy and fingerprint v3.

**Found while testing, and worth knowing.**

* **ff19SB has NO periodic torsion across N–CA or CA–C.** φ and ψ live entirely in its CMAP, so a
  backbone selection under ff19SB scales CMAP and nonbonded terms only. The fixture has an ff14SB
  variant so the φ/ψ torsion rule is exercised as well.
* Reading a `Force` from a System that has already been freed (`_nonbonded(_scaled(...))`) returns
  garbage, a charge product of 4.7e-310. This was a test bug and is fixed; it is noted because the
  pattern is easy to repeat.

**How the 0.05 kT tolerance in rows 16 and 18 was set.** The CPU platform, like CUDA mixed
precision, holds positions in single precision. Re-evaluating a stored configuration on a fresh
Context of the same platform reproduced the recorded u to 0.0077 kT absolute and 0.0055 kT across
Hamiltonians. Rounding the float64 coordinates to float32 moves u by 0.002 kT on its own.
0.05 kT is about 10× that floor. A wrong Hamiltonian misses by 8.4 kT (the region's nonbonded set
without its torsions and CMAP) to 32 kT (the whole solute), and the test asserts it is caught.

**The tolerance was set twice, and both values are recorded.** A first value of 1e-3 kT was a
guess, made before this calibration, and it assumed double-precision recomputation. It failed at
0.0057 kT, and I replaced it with the calibrated value rather than loosening it. The measurements
are in the test's docstring.

### Identity is the Hamiltonian, not its provenance (`323779f`, S0 ruling)

v3's `selection_sha256` used to hash the whole selection document, so a respelled mask or a
relabelled instance refused to resume an identical Hamiltonian. `identity.hamiltonian_selection_
projection` is now the ONE definition of the Hamiltonian-determining part:

* the hot atoms;
* the scaled and protected torsion bonds;
* each CMAP decision;
* the improper policy and the rule versions;
* per instance: residue, package, and the RESOLVED exclusions.

Resolved exclusions means the package and the named bonds, not the file's bytes: a comment in the
file is not a Hamiltonian. This is a deliberate refinement of S0's "content digest".

The identity, `ScalingSelection.digest()` and `claimed_region_differences` all go through the
projection. `scaler.yaml` also records `selection_provenance_sha256`, which is never an identity.

### G2-6b: the replacement row, REGISTERED BEFORE IT IS MEASURED (2026-09-21)

S0's ruling (`7ccc54e`): both ladders stay FAIL against G2-6 as written, and the replacement must
be DERIVED and PREDICTED rather than fitted to the two numbers that failed.

**The derivation.** In mixed precision each of the M summed energy terms carries a relative
rounding of order the single-precision epsilon. If those roundings are INDEPENDENT, the error of
the sum grows as `sqrt(M)` while the sum itself grows as `M`, so

    dU  ~  K * sqrt(|u|)            and therefore     dU/|u|  ~  K / sqrt(|u|)

An absolute kT bound assumes dU is constant in system size; a relative bound assumes it grows as
`|u|`. Both are wrong, in opposite directions, which is why 0.05 kT transferred so badly.

**K is taken from the 1,760-atom fixture ALONE**, so TYK2 tests the law instead of defining it:
`K_abs = 8.61e-5`, `K_cross = 6.49e-5` kT^(1/2).

| | predicted by the sqrt law | observed | excess |
|---|---|---|---|
| TYK2 absolute | 0.046 kT | 0.077 kT | 1.7x |
| TYK2 cross | 0.035 kT | 0.093 kT | 2.7x |

So over a 36x span in `|u|` the sqrt law is right to within a factor of 2.7, and the residual
excess is what partially CORRELATED rounding looks like (the independent-error assumption is a
lower bound on the error, never an upper one).

**The registered bound**, with a safety factor of 3 over the sqrt law:

    G2-6b passes when   dU_abs <= 3 * 8.61e-5 * sqrt(|u|)   and   dU_cross <= 3 * 6.49e-5 * sqrt(|u|)
    AND the wrong Hamiltonian misses by at least 100x the bound.

| system | `\|u\|` | bound, absolute | bound, cross |
|---|---|---|---|
| the 1,760-atom fixture | 8,000 kT | 0.023 kT | 0.017 kT |
| TYK2, 53,030 atoms | 286,191 kT | 0.138 kT | 0.104 kT |

Both existing measurements fall inside it (TYK2 by 1.8x and 1.1x; the fixture by 3x by
construction), and the discrimination margin is 10^5, far above the 100x the row requires.

**THE PREDICTION, written down before the measurement, on a system used for neither.** A third
size, intermediate between the two, run as a short selective ladder exactly as the CUDA lane's are
(a few exchanges is enough: this measures arithmetic, not sampling). For its measured `|u|` the
row predicts

    0.4 * K * sqrt(|u|)   <=   dU   <=   3 * K * sqrt(|u|)

with both halves falsifiable: BELOW the lower bound means the sqrt law overestimates and K is
wrong; ABOVE the upper bound means the error grows faster than sqrt and the whole row is refuted,
not adjusted. At `|u|` near 50,000 kT that is 0.023-0.058 kT absolute and 0.017-0.043 kT cross.

**Status: NOT MEASURED.** It needs one card for about ten minutes, requested separately rather
than by holding cards 1-4. Until it is measured and passes, **G2-6 on production-sized systems is
UNVALIDATED**, and both ladders' figures stand as measurements beside the FAIL.
